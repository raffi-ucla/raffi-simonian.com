CX Field Notes7

Requirements gathering never stops — and it starts with empathy. These are my field notes from the other side of the counter: moments where a product honored the user's time and trust, and moments where it didn't. My approach to understanding problems is grounded in empathy and emotional intelligence — feeling what the user feels before naming the pattern — because you can't fix an experience you haven't understood. Each case ends with the principle I carry into my own projects. CX here includes UX and UI: everything from an interface detail to a support channel shapes how a product treats its people.

  • ✗ FailsDead-end call to action“Chat Now” — with nothing to clickThe principle: A call to action must contain the action. Never advertise a channel without rendering its entry point.GoDaddy
  • ✗ FailsUnmonitored channelA text line that never texts backThe principle: An unstaffed channel is worse than no channel. Publish only the paths someone actually answers.GoDaddy
  • ✗ FailsAutomation as a wallAI agents that can't reach the problemThe principle: Automation should absorb the easy cases so humans get the hard ones faster — not stand between the user and resolution.GoDaddy
  • ✗ FailsError without a reason“There was an error. Please Try Again.” — for daysThe principle: An error message is an interface, not an apology. Say what failed, whose fault it is, and what to do next — or one bug becomes an endless loop.Crate & Barrel
  • ✗ FailsPromises instead of fixesSupport that declares victory — repeatedlyThe principle: Never close the loop before the problem closes. Verify the fix, then say it's fixed — a slow honest answer beats a fast false one.Crate & Barrel
  • ✗ FailsA workaround that punishes the customerThe official remedy: create a new accountThe principle: When your system fails, the remedy must cost you, not the customer. “Start over” isn't support — it's surrender, billed to the user.Crate & Barrel
  • ✓ WorksConsistency across bordersA broken iPhone in Europe, fixed like it was homeThe principle: Consistency is a feature. When a customer can predict your process anywhere in the world, trust compounds — the experience itself becomes the brand.Apple
