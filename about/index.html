About Me

An accomplished technology and business professional with an Executive MBA and more than 35 years of experience bringing technology and business solutions to startups and established organizations alike — 65+ large-scale projects managed, 20+ products launched, from enterprise to online.

I started as a software engineer — and a few years in, I realized my passion wasn’t only the technology. It was people: building communities, leading teams, and coaching others on how to put technology to work. That made me a specialist in both the core technologies and the human side of IT — and that combination has shaped everything since.

What I believe

Three convictions, from four decades on both sides — building the technology and teaching the people.

  • Digital skills

    Start with staying relevant

    I coach technologists to be business-change agents — digital evangelists, consultants, brokers, coaches, and delivery agents — not just strong programmers. Staying engaged with the business is crucial to IT’s success.

  • Business skills

    Start with why

    Instead of asking “what should we do to compete?”, ask why we started doing what we’re doing — and what could bring that cause to life with today’s technologies. People who know the why stay engaged for the long run.

  • People skills

    Start with soft skills

    Communicating bits and bytes to executives is basic miscommunication — and presenting without the big picture and business acumen is career suicide. Soft skills are what turn technical ability into influence.

My Learning & Teaching Philosophy

Eight principles that shape every program, bootcamp, and course I design.

Learning is a verb. I prioritize active learning, which allows learners to engage in higher-level thinking. Projects rely on open-ended questions that allow for critical thinking and exploration, and learners are asked to create, apply, and evaluate knowledge in ways applicable to real-world contexts.

Goals give purpose. Learners who understand the learning objectives and set their own goals are more successful. Learners are introduced to metacognitive strategies to reinforce and help achieve these goals.

Deliberate practice builds experts. I create the conditions where learners can engage in deliberate practice intent on achieving mastery of concepts and skills — with the time and opportunity necessary to support knowledge reorganization and automatization.

Neither too easy, nor too difficult. For optimal learning a sweet spot exists where the activity is neither too simple nor too difficult. Techniques like scaffolding and chunking deliver instruction that lets learners advance when ready, or find support from TAs or peers to get unblocked.

Learning is inherently social. We learn more working together than alone. Cohort collaboration leads to multifaceted understanding of complex topics, and a sense of belonging bolsters motivation, engagement, and persistence in the face of difficulties.

Guides, not sages. To achieve mastery, students need to engage actively in their learning rather than passively consume instruction. Experts, TAs, and mentors — in live sessions, communities, and cohorts — guide students toward mastery of concepts.

Without feedback there is no learning. Feedback loops and input from experts, TAs, and mentors let learners accurately identify their own growth and opportunities, and keep them on the right track — built into product experiences, instructor interactions, peer and community engagement, and project feedback.

While we teach, we learn. Teaching and mentoring introduce strong social motivations that reinforce and deepen knowledge. Learners participate in cooperative learning activities to combine knowledge; the interdependence and social responsibility this creates leads to increased effort and better outcomes.
